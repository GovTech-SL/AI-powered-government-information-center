1. Introduction 
The Department of Labour of Sri Lanka is a government institution responsible for 
administering and enforcing labour-related laws and providing services to workers, employers, 
and other stakeholders. 
The Department is involved in areas such as employment rights, labour standards, 
industrial relations, employee welfare, workplace safety, dispute resolution, and social 
security-related services. 
The Department also works to promote fair employment practices and protect the rights of 
employees while supporting employers in complying with applicable labour laws.

include: 
●​ Payment of wages

●​ Statutory leave 
●​ Maternity-related benefits 
●​ Workplace safety 
●​ Protection under labour laws 
●​ EPF-related benefits 
●​ Gratuity where applicable 
●​ The right to raise employment-related complaints

7. Employment Complaints 
Employees who believe that their employment rights have been violated may seek assistance 
from the Department of Labour.

●​ Non-payment of wages 
●​ Unlawful deductions 
●​ Employment conditions 
●​ Leave-related issues 
●​ Termination 
●​ Gratuity 
●​ EPF matters 
●​ Workplace conditions 
●​ Other labour-related disputes 
The Department may investigate or facilitate the resolution of complaints according to the 
applicable legal procedure. 
 
8. Labour Disputes 
A labour dispute may arise between an employer and an employee or between an employer 
and employees.

Examples include disputes relating to: 
●​ Wages 
●​ Employment conditions 
●​ Termination 
●​ Working conditions 
●​ Employee benefits 
●​ Other employment matters 
The Department of Labour may provide mechanisms for conciliation, dispute settlement, or 
other applicable procedures. 
9. Conciliation 
Conciliation is a process used to help parties resolve labour disputes. 
A labour officer may communicate with the parties and attempt to facilitate an acceptable 
settlement. 
The procedure depends on the nature of the dispute and the applicable labour legislation. 
 
10. Industrial Disputes 
Industrial disputes may involve disagreements between employers and employees or groups of 
employees. 
The Department may facilitate dispute-resolution mechanisms according to the applicable laws. 
Parties involved in a dispute should provide relevant information and supporting documents 
when requested.

relevant labour authority.

17. Termination of Employment 
Termination of employment is subject to applicable labour laws and regulations. 
Employees who believe that their employment has been terminated unlawfully may seek 
assistance from the relevant labour authority.

●​ Type of employment 
●​ Reason for termination 
●​ Length of employment 
●​ Applicable legislation 
●​ Size and nature of the workplace 
 
18. Gratuity 
Gratuity is an employment-related benefit that may become payable when an employee leaves 
employment, subject to applicable legal requirements. 
Eligibility may depend on factors such as: 
●​ Length of service 
●​ Nature of employment 
●​ Reason for termination 
●​ Applicable legislation 
Employees should contact the Department of Labour for the current requirements applicable to 
their situation.

A person wishing to make a labour-related complaint should provide accurate information about 
the issue. 
Information may include: 
●​ Employee name 
●​ Employer name 
●​ Workplace address 
●​ Contact information 
●​ Employment position 
●​ Employment start date 
●​ Description of the complaint 
●​ Relevant dates 
●​ Salary or wage information 
●​ Supporting documents 
The Department may request additional information during the investigation.

33. Before Making a Complaint 
Before submitting a complaint, an employee should: 
1.​ Clearly identify the employment issue. 
2.​ Collect relevant documents. 
3.​ Record important dates. 
4.​ Keep copies of communications with the employer. 
5.​ Identify the relevant workplace and employer details. 
6.​ Contact the appropriate labour office. 
7.​ Provide accurate information to the labour authority.
