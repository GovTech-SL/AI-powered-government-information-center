Mahaweli Authority of Sri Lanka: A General Overview 
Prepared November 2026 | General information summary 
1. Introduction 
The Mahaweli Authority of Sri Lanka (MASL) was established by an Act of Parliament in 1979 to implement the Mahaweli 
Development Programme, the country's largest multipurpose development effort. The programme set out to divert the 
waters of the Mahaweli Ganga, Sri Lanka's longest river, to the Dry Zone for irrigation, hydropower and new settlements. 
It involved building reservoirs, hydropower plants and irrigation networks, and developing large areas of land for 
agriculture.

3. Historical Background 
• Ancient irrigation: Sri Lanka's ancient hydraulic civilisation diverted the Mahaweli Ganga and its main branch, the 
Amban Ganga, at places such as Minipe and Elahera. 
• 1900: irrigation engineer W. T. Strange first recommended diverting the Mahaweli Ganga to the Dry Zone. 
• 1968 Master Plan: prepared by the UNDP and FAO, it proposed irrigating 900,000 acres, generating hydropower 
with an installed capacity of 507 MW, in three phases over 30 years at an estimated Rs. 6,700 million (1968 prices). 
• 1970: the foundation stone was laid. Stage I covered the Polgolla and Bowatenne diversions and tunnels. 
• Accelerated Mahaweli Programme (after 1977): built the Kotmale, Victoria, Randenigala and Maduru Oya dams, 
aiming to develop about 320,000 acres of new land and 80,000 acres of existing irrigated land, settle about 140,000 
new settlers with their families, and make the country self-sufficient in rice, with hydropower and jobs as further 
goals. 
4. Mandate and Functions 
The Authority's current task is to implement the Master Plan in the remaining and gazetted areas. This includes 
rehabilitating and maintaining the irrigation network, administering land, enhancing agricultural production, supporting 
settlers after settlement, and managing irrigation water. Hydropower from the irrigation releases is generated and 
operated by the Ceylon Electricity Board. The Authority's stated vision is to be Sri Lanka's best organisation in using land 
and water for innovative agriculture, renewable energy, environmental conservation and better living standards for 
citizens. 
5. Organisation 
• Governed by a Board of Directors and led by the Director General, with divisions including Land Use Planning, the 
Secretariat of Water Management, Business Development, Administration and Environment. 
• Regional offices in the Mahaweli zones are headed by Residential Project Managers. 
• As listed on the Authority's website, the parent ministry is led by Minister K. D. Lal Kantha, Deputy Minister of Lands 
and Irrigation Aravinda Senarath, and Secretary D. P. Wickramasinghe.

6. Key Programmes and Recent Developments 
• Watershed and water resources: ten major projects were launched in August 2025 under the Integrated Watershed 
and Water Resources Management Project (IWWRMP). 
• Mahaweli Water Security Investment Program (MWSIP), Stage 2: tenders were called in 2026 for works such as 
stream and anicut improvements. 
• Farm leasing: a January 2026 Cabinet decision provides for leasing Authority-run farms to farmers for cultivation, 
with land ownership remaining with the Authority. 
• Land administration: the Authority handles land matters in Mahaweli special areas, including ownership inquiries 
under the Act (for example, a July 2026 gazette notice for the B Zone). 
• Tourism and heritage: holiday bungalows and the Mahaweli Museum, alongside the Authority's renewable energy 
and environment goals. 
7. Key Challenges 
Climate variability and water scarcity; ageing irrigation infrastructure; land tenure and administration; conserving forests 
in the upper catchment; and sustaining livelihoods and productivity in established settlements.
